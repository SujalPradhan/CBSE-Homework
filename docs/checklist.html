How this was designed

Short version. Open a section for more.

What Anand asked for
  • Practical tasks are fine. They must be online.
  • The teacher must have no extra load. A system verifies.
  • Students may use AI or work together and still learn.
How a result is decided
  • Measured numbers must be plausible.
  • Calculated numbers must agree with the student’s own measurements.
  • “Use your number on a new case” answers are checked against the student’s own set number.
  • Choices can block a top result but never grant one.
  • One sentence is checked by an AI for meaning. It can lower the result, never raise it.
  • The result is a signed code. Nothing is stored.
What was tested (14 Oct 2026)

Small test set written by us. No real students yet.

TestResult
30 labelled sheets, 5 tasksAll as expected: honest sheets Got it; shallow, wrong, keyword and injected sheets never Got it
Red-team, 41 checksNo crashes. No forged or upgraded codes. All 6 injection attempts failed.
Fixed after the red-teamWrong choices now block Got it. Retry cap no longer resets with the set number. Set number is signed into the code. Tighter fresh-case tolerance.

Model: GPT-4.1-mini. The smaller nano model was too noisy.

What it cannot do
  • It cannot prove the activity was done. An invented but consistent sheet reached Got it on 5 of 5 tasks.
  • A student can learn the right answer by re-checking many times. A daily cap slows this down.
  • A lightly reworded copy gets a different code.
  • A teacher who wants certainty can ask the 2-minute check on the teacher page.
Evidence behind the design
  • NCF-SE 2023 §3.3.7: homework should apply learning to different contexts. It warns about AI doing homework. PDF
  • Unrestricted GPT-4 raised practice scores 48% but lowered later exam scores 17% (Turkey, about 1,000 students). Bastani et al. 2025
  • Predicting before a demonstration improved understanding. Crouch et al. 2004
  • Homework links positively with achievement, more so in Grades 7–12 (US, flawed designs). Cooper et al. 2006
  • 82% of rural 14–16-year-olds can use a smartphone; 57% of those used it for study last week. ASER 2024
  • Gap: CBSE’s own circulars on the new NCERT books could not be retrieved (404).
Checklist for a new task
  • MUSTNames its NCERT chapter and activity.
  • MUSTOne step only the student can do.
  • MUSTSafe, private, and uses things found at home.
  • MUSTWorks with no teacher effort.
  • GOODUnder 25 minutes, readable by a Grade 6 student.
  • GOODWrong answers do not give away the right one.
Pilot plan
  • 2–3 teachers, one class each, one task a week for 3 weeks.
  • Measure: the 2-minute check score, teacher minutes spent, and who could not finish and why.
  • Compare with a similar class given the usual worksheet.
